Manuscript Framework 
Capstone Manuscript Framework
Based on the STI Capstone Project Manual and manuscript templates, here is the updated general outline for Chapters I to IV, including the integrated survey and implementation flow, followed by a comprehensive list of Appendices.
Chapter I: Introduction
This chapter establishes the context, problem, and theoretical basis for your project.
A.	Project Context: Presents the "big picture" of the problem, describing existing conditions or prevailing situations that necessitate the project.
B.	Capstone Project Questions: Specific, relevant questions that provide direction to the project and indicate what you aim to discover.
C.	Objectives of the Project: Describes the project's purpose, how it improves practice, and how the client or society will benefit.
D.	Scope, Limitations, and Delimitations: Defines the extent of the project, factors within its range, and boundaries such as location, time period, or population.
E.	Review of Related Literature: A synthesis of established ideas and prior research from published and unpublished materials related to your topic.
F.	Theoretical/Conceptual Framework: Defines the theory (Theoretical) or the researcher’s explanation of concept relationships (Conceptual) often illustrated via a diagram.
Chapter II: Methodology
This chapter describes the techniques and processes used to gather data and develop the solution.
A.	Sample/Population: Characteristics of the research subjects and the selection process for your surveys.
B.	Data Gathering Tools: Details the equipment and instruments used, such as pre- and post-survey questionnaires and interview guides.
C.	Data Gathering Procedures: Explains how surveys were conducted, how data was retrieved, and the statistical tests used for analysis.
D.	Ethical Considerations: Describes how you ensured the confidentiality and data privacy of your participants.
E.	Development Components: For multimedia projects, this includes Requirement Analysis (product features), Design, and Testing/Evaluation protocols.
Chapter III: Results
This chapter presents the gathered data using visuals to highlight values that answer your research questions.
A.	Profile of Research Subjects: Relevant demographic information about the participants.
B.	Pre-Project Survey Results: Presentation of baseline data (using tables and charts) gathered before implementation to establish the initial state of the problem.
C.	Project Implementation: Documentation of the actual deployment of your project.
a.	Implementation Plan: How the output (film, exhibit, etc.) was installed or screened.
b.	Site-Specific Requirements: Resources used for rollout (e.g., equipment or software).
D.	Post-Project Survey/Implementation Results: Data gathered after implementation to serve as a ground for evaluating the project's success.
a.	Includes tabulated results and summarized evidence like direct quotations from transcripts.
Chapter IV: Discussion
This chapter interprets the findings and provides the final significance of the work.
A.	Interpretation of Data: Explains what the data from Chapter III implies regarding the problem.
B.	Clear Answers to Project Questions: Provides the definitive answers to the questions posed in Chapter I.
C.	Comparison with Literature: Discusses how your results agree or disagree with other published works.
D.	Closing Summary: A final statement on the overall significance of the project output.
E.	Recommendations: Suggested improvements for implementation, ways to address limitations, and future research or technical enhancements.

List of Appendices
The Appendices contain supporting documents and the technical "making of" details of your project.
1.	Project and Content Development Brief: A mandated document for multimedia projects detailing the chosen medium, technical production standards, and creative development (pre-prod to post-prod).
2.	Research Instrument: Copies of your pre- and post-survey questionnaires or interview guides.
3.	Correspondence: Letters to clients, participants, or local authorities Transcriptions: Full records of interviews or focus group discussions (if applicable).
4.	Gantt Chart of Activities: The project's timeline and schedule.
5.	Actual Capstone Project Expenses: A breakdown of costs incurred during the project.
6.	User’s Manual/Guide: Instructions for the client or audience on how to interact with the project or system.
7.	Client/Resource Person Information: Contact details and profiles of project stakeholders.
8.	Certification of Originality Check: Proof that the manuscript was checked for plagiarism.
9.	Certification of Editing: Proof that the paper was professionally proofread.
10.	Certification of Statistical Treatment: (If applicable) validation from a statistician.
11.	Biographical Statement: Short professional profiles of the researchers.
12.	Technical Attachments: May include relevant source codes, sample input/output reports, or high-resolution screenshots of the final project.
13.	AI Declaration: a one-page letter indicating the brevity and scope of AI usage.
 
Project Timeline 
Capstone Project Timeline
Based on the STI Capstone Project manual and course outlines, here is a suggested schedule for your Capstone Project 2 term. This schedule integrates the pre-survey, project implementation, and post-survey workflow into the standard academic timeline.
Phase 1: Research Readiness & Baseline Data
January 19 – February 8 (Weeks 1 to 3)
●	January 19: Capstone Project 2 Orientation. Review research guidelines and set work goals.
●	Weeks 1-2: Review your approved proposal and Project and Content Development Brief.
●	Week 2: Instrument Development. Finalize your survey questionnaires (pre and post) and interview guides.
●	Week 3: Pre-Project Survey Gathering. Conduct data gathering to establish the baseline awareness or existing conditions before your intervention.
●	Deliverable: Validated research instruments and baseline data.
Phase 2: Project Production & Early Manuscript Drafting
February 9 – March 22 (Weeks 4 to 9)
●	Weeks 4-7: Project Production (Prototyping). Focus on the technical and creative aspects of your short film, photography, or social media content.
●	Week 8: Internal Testing. Test your multimedia prototype to ensure it aligns with your Project Brief.
●	Week 4-9: Manuscript Drafting (Chapter 3). Begin writing Chapter III (Results) by presenting your subject profiles and baseline pre-survey findings.
●	Deliverable: Finished multimedia prototype and drafted pre-project results.
Phase 3: Implementation & Post-Data Gathering
March 23 – April 12 (Weeks 10 to 12)
●	Week 10: Project Implementation (The Rollout). This is your "intervention" phase—holding the exhibit, screening the film, or launching the campaign.
●	Week 11: Post-Project Survey Gathering. Collect data immediately after your rollout to measure changes in awareness or perception.
●	Week 12: Data Analysis. Analyze the difference between your pre- and post-project results.
●	Deliverable: Implementation evidence (photos/metrics) and raw post-project data.
Phase 4: Discussion & Endorsement
April 13 – April 25 (Weeks 13 to 14)
●	Week 13: Finalizing Manuscript (Chapter 4). Write Chapter IV (Discussion), providing clear answers to your project questions by comparing pre and post data.
●	Week 14: Final Editing & Appendices. Compile all appendices, including your mandated Project and Content Development Brief.
●	April 22/25 (One week before defense): Submission for Defense. You must give your finished working output and paper to the panel one week before the target date.
●	Deliverable: Completed Manuscript (Chapters 1-4) and Final Multimedia Output.
Phase 5: Defense & Finalization
April 27 – May 18 (Weeks 15 to 18)
●	April 29/May 2: TARGET FINAL DEFENSE DATE. Defend your manuscript and product before the panel in corporate attire.
●	May 3–10: Revisions. Complete any adjustments requested by the panel (usually within 5–7 days).
●	May 18: Final Submission. Submit the hardbound copy to the coordinator and upload the soft copy to the library.
Key Weekly Requirement
Throughout this entire schedule, you are required to have at least one (1) hour of weekly consultation with your Capstone Project Adviser to ensure your production is aligned with your research.

Best Practices 
Best Practices Guidelines

Category	Best Practice & Requirement	
Group Management	Maintain Group Size: Ensure the group has a minimum of 4 and a maximum of 5 members.	
	Define Roles: Clearly identify and document the specific roles and responsibilities of each group member.	
	Conduct Peer Evaluations: Use peer evaluation forms to maintain accountability and assess individual contributions.	
Stakeholder Engagement	Weekly Adviser Meetings: Meet with your Capstone Project Adviser for at least one (1) hour of consultation every week.	
	Document Consultations: Consistently update the Capstone Project Consultation Form and have it signed during every meeting.	
	Vet Your Client: Secure an external client willing to commit for two semesters and provide access to necessary data.	
Research & Ethics	Establish a Baseline: Always conduct pre-project data gathering (surveys/interviews) to establish the current conditions of the problem.	
	Prioritize Privacy: Ensure informed consent and strictly maintain the confidentiality and data privacy of all participants.	
	Check Feasibility: Continuously evaluate if the project is achievable within your financial resources and time constraints.	
Documentation & Formatting	Follow Official Formatting: Use Times New Roman, size 12 font, 1.5 line spacing, and specific margins (1.5" left, 1" others).	
	Align with the Project Brief: Ensure the actual multimedia output remains strictly aligned with the approved Project and Content Development Brief.	
	Visualize Data: In Chapter 3, use well-thought-out tables and charts accompanied by descriptive text to highlight relevant values.	
	APA Compliance: Use the latest edition of APA format for all in-text citations and the reference list.	
Production & Implementation	Document the Rollout: Clearly detail the implementation plan, including site-specific requirements and deployment steps in Chapter III.	
	Highlight Evidence: Use direct quotations from interview transcripts or summarized evidence to support your findings.	
Defense Readiness	Advance Submission: Submit your finished working output and the manuscript to the panel one (1) week before the defense.	
	Professional Conduct: Attend the final defense in corporate or business attire.	
	Address Feedback: Be prepared to complete all revisions requested by the panel within 5 to 7 calendar days after the defense.	
Final Archiving	Physical & Digital Submission: Submit one (1) hardbound copy to the library and upload a soft copy to the official STI Research Outputs library.
