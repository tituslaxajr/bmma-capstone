Capstone Manuscript Framework
Based on the STI Capstone Project Manual and manuscript templates, here is the updated general outline for Chapters I to IV, including the integrated survey and implementation flow, followed by a comprehensive list of Appendices.
Chapter I: Introduction
This chapter establishes the context, problem, and theoretical basis for your project.
Project Context: Presents the "big picture" of the problem, describing existing conditions or prevailing situations that necessitate the project.
Capstone Project Questions: Specific, relevant questions that provide direction to the project and indicate what you aim to discover.
Objectives of the Project: Describes the project's purpose, how it improves practice, and how the client or society will benefit.
Scope, Limitations, and Delimitations: Defines the extent of the project, factors within its range, and boundaries such as location, time period, or population.
Review of Related Literature: A synthesis of established ideas and prior research from published and unpublished materials related to your topic.
Theoretical/Conceptual Framework: Defines the theory (Theoretical) or the researcher’s explanation of concept relationships (Conceptual) often illustrated via a diagram.
Chapter II: Methodology
This chapter describes the techniques and processes used to gather data and develop the solution.
Sample/Population: Characteristics of the research subjects and the selection process for your surveys.
Data Gathering Tools: Details the equipment and instruments used, such as pre- and post-survey questionnaires and interview guides.
Data Gathering Procedures: Explains how surveys were conducted, how data was retrieved, and the statistical tests used for analysis.
Ethical Considerations: Describes how you ensured the confidentiality and data privacy of your participants.
Development Components: For multimedia projects, this includes Requirement Analysis (product features), Design, and Testing/Evaluation protocols.
Chapter III: Results
This chapter presents the gathered data using visuals to highlight values that answer your research questions.
Profile of Research Subjects: Relevant demographic information about the participants.
Pre-Project Survey Results: Presentation of baseline data (using tables and charts) gathered before implementation to establish the initial state of the problem.
Project Implementation: Documentation of the actual deployment of your project.
Implementation Plan: How the output (film, exhibit, etc.) was installed or screened.
Site-Specific Requirements: Resources used for rollout (e.g., equipment or software).
Post-Project Survey/Implementation Results: Data gathered after implementation to serve as a ground for evaluating the project's success.
Includes tabulated results and summarized evidence like direct quotations from transcripts.
Chapter IV: Discussion
This chapter interprets the findings and provides the final significance of the work.
Interpretation of Data: Explains what the data from Chapter III implies regarding the problem.
Clear Answers to Project Questions: Provides the definitive answers to the questions posed in Chapter I.
Comparison with Literature: Discusses how your results agree or disagree with other published works.
Closing Summary: A final statement on the overall significance of the project output.
Recommendations: Suggested improvements for implementation, ways to address limitations, and future research or technical enhancements.

List of Appendices
The Appendices contain supporting documents and the technical "making of" details of your project.
Project and Content Development Brief: A mandated document for multimedia projects detailing the chosen medium, technical production standards, and creative development (pre-prod to post-prod).
Research Instrument: Copies of your pre- and post-survey questionnaires or interview guides.
Correspondence: Letters to clients, participants, or local authorities Transcriptions: Full records of interviews or focus group discussions (if applicable).
Gantt Chart of Activities: The project's timeline and schedule.
Actual Capstone Project Expenses: A breakdown of costs incurred during the project.
User’s Manual/Guide: Instructions for the client or audience on how to interact with the project or system.
Client/Resource Person Information: Contact details and profiles of project stakeholders.
Certification of Originality Check: Proof that the manuscript was checked for plagiarism.
Certification of Editing: Proof that the paper was professionally proofread.
Certification of Statistical Treatment: (If applicable) validation from a statistician.
Biographical Statement: Short professional profiles of the researchers.
Technical Attachments: May include relevant source codes, sample input/output reports, or high-resolution screenshots of the final project.
AI Declaration: a one-page letter indicating the brevity and scope of AI usage.
